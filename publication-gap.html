The Inspection Publication Gap

Introduction

The visit ends. The wait begins.

When an inspection visit finishes, schools, parents and ministries are waiting for the same thing: the report. Between the last day on site and publication sit evidence collation, report writing, quality assurance and sign-off.

We collected the timelines that national and regional inspectorates publish themselves, in handbooks, statistical notes, budget documents and official releases. Every figure below was checked against the source text on the date shown in the sources section. We present published targets and expectations as they are written. They are not measures of how any body performs, and they are not like-for-like: start points, units and stages differ, so each bar says what it measures.

We found verified published figures for 12 inspectorates. 8 gave a timeline that could be compared on a chart. Where we could not verify a figure on the source page, we left it out.

Comparison

Published timelines, side by side

Bars are drawn to scale within each chart. The number beside each bar is the figure as published. Superscript numbers link to the sources.

Weeks are converted at 5 working days a week where noted (Education Scotland, BQA), which is our arithmetic. Start points differ: Ofsted and Estyn count from the end of the inspection, ETI from the spoken report-back, ISI from the inspection, Chile from the day after the last day of the visit. Uganda's 7 days is the published deadline for inspectors to submit the report, as reported by the press. ISI's figure is from its British Schools Overseas handbook. ETI's guidance is dated 2018 and may have been updated since. Ofsted's published expectation is from March 2022; its September 2023 handbook sets 18 working days to the draft, 30 to the final report and 5 more to publication.2

Takeaways

Three things the published figures show

1. Targets cluster in a 20 day band

Among the 5 published targets to publication, the range is 30 working days (ETI) to 50 working days (Education Scotland, 10 working weeks). Ofsted sits at 38 and Estyn at 45.1

2. The draft stage varies most

Published draft or first report timings run from a 7 day submission deadline in Uganda to 26 business days in Chile, with Ofsted at 18 working days and BQA at 5 weeks. These measure different things, which is itself a finding: there is no common yardstick.

3. On-time performance is rarely published

Chile publishes an on-time rate, a 93% target for 2026 against 99.25% in 2024.7 Education Scotland disclosed that 866 reports were published later than 10 weeks since 2011.4 Ofsted has published an average of 27 working days for April to July 2021.1 For most others we found no performance figure.

What drives the gap

Four stages between visit and publication

Published timelines name these stages. None of the documents we read breaks the time down by stage, so the days are not allocated here.

1. Evidence collation

Observations, interviews and notes from several inspectors are brought together after the visit.

2. Report writing

Findings are drafted section by section. ISI's overseas timeline has the draft and evidence submitted 5 days after the inspection.6

3. Quality assurance

The same ISI timeline lists quality assurance of the evidence and draft report as its own step before the draft goes to the school.6

4. Sign-off and factual accuracy

Schools check the draft for factual accuracy. Estyn normally allows 5 working days; ISI gives 5 term-time days.6 Final approval follows, then publication.

One arithmetic point from the published ISI timeline: the final report is issued at day 30 and the draft is submitted at day 5, so 25 days sit between draft submission and issue. The school response window accounts for 5 of them. The handbook does not itemise the rest.6
