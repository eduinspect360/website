How we calculate this

The method is published, and the assumptions are yours to change.

The model counts direct inspector time only. It leaves out travel, overheads, school side time and the value of earlier publication, so the figures stay conservative.

Our planning assumptions

Formulas

  1. Writing days per inspection: W = inspectors per inspection × writing days per inspector
  2. Report writing days back: Dw = inspections × W × sw
  3. Quality assurance days back: Dq = inspections × 1/3 day × sq. A quality assurance day covers about 3 reports.2
  4. Inspector days back to classrooms: Dw + Dq
  5. Extra inspections possible: (Dw + Dq) ÷ (inspectors × on-site days + W + 1/3). The divisor is the full inspector time one inspection takes: on site, writing and review.4
  6. Value of time redeployed: Dw × day cost + Dq × quality assurance day cost. The quality assurance day cost is your day cost × 1.17, the ratio of the published rates of 392 and 335.2
  7. Working days sooner to published results: days from visit to published × (18 ÷ 38) × st. The drafting window is 18 of the 38 working days in England's published timeline.1, 4

Worked example with the defaults

1,000 inspections, 2 inspectors each on site for 2 days and writing for 1 day (W = 2 days), a day cost of £335 and 38 working days to publication. In the expected case, Dw is 1,400 days and Dq is 167 days, so 1,567 inspector days return to classrooms. One inspection takes 2 × 2 + 2 + 1/3 = 6.33 inspector days, so that is 247 extra inspections, worth about £534,000 a year, with reports published 10.8 working days sooner.

Sources

  1. Ofsted, methodology for state-funded schools inspections and outcomes as at 31 December 2021, GOV.UK: reports are published "within 38 working days of the end of the inspection".
  2. Ofsted, Payments for contracted Ofsted inspectors, GOV.UK, updated 26 June 2025: £335 a day for a team inspector and £392 a day for quality assurance, where a full day covers the quality assurance of an average of 3 inspection outputs.
  3. UK Government, "Landmark government trial shows AI could save civil servants nearly 2 weeks a year", GOV.UK, 2 June 2025: an average of 26 minutes a day saved across more than 20,000 civil servants, self-reported.
  4. Ofsted, School inspection handbook for September 2023 (now withdrawn): graded and ungraded inspections "normally last 2 days", and the draft report goes to the school within 18 working days of the end of the inspection.
