How we calculate this

The method is published, and the assumptions are yours to change.

The model counts direct inspector time only. It leaves out travel, overheads, school side time and the value of earlier publication, so the figures stay conservative.

Reporting at scale is where inspector time goes. Dubai inspected 209 private schools in 2023-24.6 Sharjah's first Itqan round assessed 110 of its 127 private schools, as reported by GEMS Education.7 Published targets for time to publication run from 30 to 50 working days across the inspectorates we checked.5 England is one example among several, and every figure here can be replaced with your own.

Formulas

  1. Writing days per inspection: W = inspectors per inspection × writing days per inspector
  2. Report writing days back: Dw = inspections × W × sw
  3. Quality assurance days back: Dq = inspections × 1/3 day × sq. This is a planning assumption based on England's published figure that a quality assurance day covers about 3 reports.2 Replace it with your own.
  4. Inspector days freed for inspection: Dw + Dq
  5. Extra inspections possible: (Dw + Dq) ÷ (inspectors × on-site days + W + 1/3). The divisor is the full inspector time one inspection takes: on site, writing and review.4
  6. Value of time freed: Dw × day cost + Dq × quality assurance day cost. This only shows once you enter a day cost. The quality assurance day cost is your day cost × 1.17, a planning assumption based on the ratio of England's published rates of 392 and 335.2 It rests on one published source, so replace it if your own ratio differs.
  7. Working days sooner to published results: days from visit to published × (18 ÷ 38) × st. The drafting window is about 47% of the time from visit to publication (18 of 38 working days in England's published timeline). This is a planning assumption, so replace it with your own.1, 4

Worked example with the planning defaults

1,000 inspections, 2 inspectors each on site for 2 days and writing for 1 day (W = 2 days), 38 working days to publication, and, as an example only, a day cost of GBP 335 (the England published rate). In the expected case, Dw is 1,400 days and Dq is 167 days, so 1,567 inspector days are freed for inspection. One inspection takes 2 × 2 + 2 + 1/3 = 6.33 inspector days, so that is 247 extra inspections, worth about GBP 534,000 a year, with reports published 10.8 working days sooner. The days, extra inspections and days sooner do not depend on the day cost.
